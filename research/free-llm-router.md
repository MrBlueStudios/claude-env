# free-llm-router
**Question:** Which GitHub project is behind the reel's "7 billion free tokens a month across 600 models" claim, and is pooling free LLM tiers through it safe and allowed for a four-person EU studio doing non-secret bulk summarising?
**Answer:** The project is FreeLLMAPI (tashfeenahmed/freellmapi), a real MIT-licensed self-hosted router, and the reel's 7.4B figure is a theoretical sum of per-provider free allowances, not a usable pool. Don't run the router: it is documented "personal experimentation only", its multi-key rotation can breach provider terms, and several tiers train on prompts or have turned paid. For public transcripts, use Groq directly, optionally with Cloudflare Workers AI as a second provider.
**Confidence:** Medium. The identification and the provider terms are verified from primary pages, but Google's current free limits are console-only and several provider details are UNVERIFIED.
**What would change our decision:**
- A provider moves its free tier to paid or card-gated (Cerebras already did). Then drop it, or budget for it as a deliberate purchase.
- The studio wants to send private or client material. Then no free tier qualifies, and a paid zero-retention EU option is needed instead.

## Findings

1. **The project.** The best match is [FreeLLMAPI (tashfeenahmed/freellmapi)](https://github.com/tashfeenahmed/freellmapi). Licence is MIT. The page showed 57.9k stars, 8.3k forks and 1,175 commits on `main`. The last-commit date was not shown in what I could read, so it is UNVERIFIED. It is real router software, not a list. It is a self-hosted, OpenAI-compatible `/v1` gateway with routing, failover and key rotation, written in TypeScript and run via Docker or Node. You bring your own free keys. The README's About line says "Personal experimentation only". I could not read the linked Disclaimer text, so it is UNVERIFIED. Whether the router actually works as claimed is UNVERIFIED, because I did not read the code.

2. **The figure.** The reel's numbers match the [project page on neu.ie](https://neu.ie/work/freellmapi/): "605 free LLMs from 34 providers", "roughly 7.4 billion free tokens a month", marked updated September 2026. The README now says 10.1B tokens, 736 model families and 87 providers. Neither page states how the number is derived. It is most likely a sum of every model's nominal monthly free allowance, which is my inference and is UNVERIFIED. It is not a pool you can actually use. Per-minute and per-day caps bind first, and each model on a provider shares that provider's limits. Older versions and forks quoted about 1.7B tokens a month across 16 providers ([joshcohen-ai/free-ai-tokens](https://github.com/joshcohen-ai/free-ai-tokens)), so the headline grows as providers are added.

3. **Other candidates.** [cheahjs/free-llm-api-resources](https://github.com/cheahjs/free-llm-api-resources) (about 29k stars) is a list of free tiers, not a router. It would be the source for the limits, not the thing the reel describes. [PryxIntel/free-llm-api](https://github.com/PryxIntel/free-llm-api) says "34+ providers & 600+ models" but has 3 stars, 8 commits and unsourced limits. It looks like SEO spam. Reject both as the reel's project.

4. **Rotation across keys.** FreeLLMAPI tracks rate limits per `(platform, model, key)` and does "key rotation", so it implies several keys per provider. That is the pattern the terms below restrict.

6. **Terms on pooling.** The only explicit bans I could verify are on the multi-account side. OpenRouter §7.3 prohibits "multiple accounts ... for purposes of bypassing or circumventing use limits". Groq's archived Saudi terms (an older regional page, not its current agreement) bar circumventing free limits. One account per provider, called through a router, was not prohibited in the terms I read. FreeLLMAPI's multi-key rotation is the part that can cross the line. Resale bans (Groq §6.3c, OpenRouter §7.4) are not triggered by internal use. Google, Cerebras, Mistral, NVIDIA and Cloudflare multi-account clauses were not verified.

7. **Production and commercial use.** The Groq agreement covers businesses and developers ("not for consumer use") and caps liability for free services at $5,000. NVIDIA's trial bars production. Google says AI Studio is for professional or business purposes, but requires paid services for EEA end users. Summarising transcripts for internal use is not "serving end users". Anything shipped in a client product would be.

8. **EU/EEA/UK carve-outs.** Google is the one with a verified regional rule, and it points the opposite way from the cheap summary. Google's terms do not say "no training for EU users on free". They say only Paid Services may be offered to users in the EEA, Switzerland or the UK. [freellmapi.co's summary](https://freellmapi.co/free-llm-api-data-training) reads it differently, and is a secondary source from the router project itself. No carve-out was found for Groq, Cloudflare, OpenRouter or Mistral. Groq holds data in the US under SCCs.

9. **Verdict for non-secret bulk work.** Summarising public video transcripts is low-risk on data, since nothing private is sent. The risks are bans and tiers turning paid. Smallest safe setup:
   - One Groq account, called directly with the OpenAI-compatible endpoint, plus a retry-with-backoff on 429. Add Cloudflare Workers AI as a fallback only if needed.
   - No router, no extra accounts, no Google, OpenRouter `:free`, NVIDIA or Cerebras.
   - Realistic capacity is well under a million tokens a day, nowhere near "7 billion a month". If that is not enough, budget a small paid plan on Groq or Cloudflare instead of stacking free tiers.
   - Free tiers change fast: Cerebras and GitHub Models both changed within about four months, and Gemini's free quota dropped sharply. Re-check before relying on any of them.
