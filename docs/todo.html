Connect Jira to Google Calendar

your stated next move · a click path through two GUIs you own

You said on 17 November this is what you want next, and asked what should appear. The board is your own: blitsol.atlassian.net, project key KAN.

Recommendation: due dates only, assigned to you, one feed. And one of the three options on the old version of this card does not apply: you created the board from the Kanban template, so there are no sprint boundaries to show unless you deliberately turn sprints on. What decides whether this works is not the feed at all. It is what a due date means on a board four people write to. Agree that first, with the other three, or the feed is either empty or noise.

The convention worth agreeing: a due date means an outside party expects it on that date. A client deadline, a filing date, a launch. Internal sequencing is expressed by column and priority and never by a due date. That keeps the calendar sparse enough that every entry means something, and it matches where your actual risk sits. Forgetting internal work is what these boards are for. The thing that hurts is an outside date arriving unnoticed, and you already have three of those: Rovo on 3 December, the Moneybird trial before it bills, and the CLM revisit around 8 December.

  1. Write the due-date convention down and tell the other three. One sentence is enough. Done when all four of you would answer "should this get a due date?" the same way.
  2. Put due dates on the outside dates you already have, so there is something real to look at. Done when the board has at least three issues with due dates that you did not invent for the test.
  3. Make a saved filter rather than subscribing to the whole board: project = KAN AND assignee = currentUser() AND duedate IS NOT EMPTY ORDER BY duedate. Done when the filter returns exactly the issues you would want to see coming.
  4. Subscribe to that filter to get an iCal URL, then Google Calendar → Other calendars → + → From URL. Done when one real deadline shows on the right day. Treat the URL as a secret: anyone holding it reads the filter with no login. Expect to hunt for the subscribe option, since Atlassian moves it.
  5. Give it a colour you read as "the board's, not mine", and leave it one week before judging it. Done when you can say whether you looked at it. If you did not, the problem is the convention in step 1, not the integration.

What I would not put in, and why: every issue with a due date regardless of assignee. There is a real argument for it, since you are the one who unblocks the other three, but it belongs in a second feed in a second colour added once you know the volume, not in the first one. A four-person board's worth of dates in your personal calendar on day one is how you learn to ignore it. Also leave out status changes and new issues: those are stream-shaped, not day-shaped, and the board already shows them.

Looks like success, isn't: Google refreshes subscribed iCal feeds on its own schedule, often hours behind and sometimes a full day. That is normal, but it means the feed is wrong for anything same-day, and if the crew starts relying on it the answer is the Atlassian MCP plus a real sync, not a faster refresh.
